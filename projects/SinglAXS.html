SinglAXS Quadruped

Experimental 1-DOF quadruped robot exploring gait generation, locomotion stability, and minimal-actuation walking systems.

SinglAXS was built to investigate how much walking functionality could be achieved using an intentionally constrained mechanical design. Instead of relying on complex inverse kinematics and multi-axis legs, the platform used a single rotational axis per leg with direct-drive SG90 servos.

Project Overview

Traditional quadruped robots commonly use multiple degrees of freedom per leg alongside advanced motion planning systems. SinglAXS intentionally reduced this complexity to explore how stable walking behavior could emerge from a highly constrained platform.

The robot used four SG90 servos, a lightweight 3D printed frame, and an ESP32-WROVER microcontroller running FreeRTOS-based gait logic. The project focused primarily on gait experimentation, synchronization, and mechanical iteration rather than creating a polished final product.

Design Goals

Minimal Actuation

Each leg used only a single rotational axis to simplify control logic and reduce overall mechanical complexity.

Compact Architecture

The frame was optimized around lightweight 3D printed parts and direct-drive SG90 servos.

Gait Experimentation

The project emphasized timing, phase relationships, and locomotion stability testing.

System Architecture

Hardware

The robot used a lightweight embedded system architecture designed around low cost and rapid iteration.

  • ESP32-WROVER Microcontroller
  • 4x SG90 Servo Motors
  • MPU6050 IMU
  • 3D Printed Chassis
  • Direct-drive leg mechanism

Firmware

Motion sequencing and gait timing were implemented using FreeRTOS. Different gait patterns were tested by modifying timing offsets, servo synchronization, and stepping sequences.

The firmware architecture prioritized modular gait testing and rapid iteration over advanced autonomy features.

Mechanical Design

The mechanical structure was designed around the limitations of SG90 servos and single-axis leg movement. Emphasis was placed on keeping the frame lightweight while maintaining enough rigidity for repeatable gait experiments.

Several iterations explored changes to body geometry, leg spacing, and mass placement to improve walking stability.

Gait Development

Crawl Gait

Crawl-style gait patterns prioritized stability and continuous ground contact. These tests were useful for evaluating balance and servo synchronization under slower movement conditions.

Trot Experiments

Faster trot-inspired stepping sequences improved forward speed but introduced stability challenges due to the robot's limited mechanical degrees of freedom.

Website Interface

Control & Monitoring Interface

A lightweight browser-based interface was developed to assist with testing and system interaction during development. The interface was not only used for rapid iteration, gait tuning, and debugging during walking experiments, but also allowed for manual control of the robot.

The UI prioritized simplicity and responsiveness, allowing adjustments to movement behavior without repeatedly modifying firmware parameters directly in code.

Debugging & Iteration

Servo Jitter

One of the primary technical challenges involved servo jitter and timing inconsistencies during synchronized movement.

Multiple firmware revisions explored different update strategies and gait timing approaches to reduce instability.

Mechanical Revisions

Iterative modifications in leg design were used to analyze the optimal geometry for stability, traction, and weight.
